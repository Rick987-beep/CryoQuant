# The Credit Crunch Fund

**A five-year expression built for a rare credit crisis.**

---

## The idea

You commit **$5 million** for five years. If a serious dislocation hits North American investment-grade corporate bonds during that period, our research indicates the fund can return **ten times your capital or more** in the crisis window. In calm years the position is expected to **cost** money, and that is intentional rather than a flaw in the design.

This is not a balanced portfolio. It is **catastrophe insurance paid in advance**, structured so that a genuine credit crunch can produce a very large payoff relative to the capital posted.

---

## What we trade

We buy **protection** on the **CDX.NA.IG** index.

You can think of it as insurance on the collective health of large U.S. investment-grade companies. It is not a bet on a single stock or a narrow sector. When **markets are calm**, that insurance costs money to hold, much like an annual premium on a property policy. When **credit comes under stress**, spreads widen and lenders demand higher yields, and the value of our protection **rises**.

We earn when fear replaces complacency in corporate bond markets.

**Instrument:** CDX.NA.IG, five-year index credit default swap (CDS)  
**Position:** Long protection (we benefit when credit stress worsens)  
**Your capital:** **$5,000,000** posted as collateral for a five-year fund term

## How the payoff works

| Market mood | What happens to our position | What it means for you |
|-------------|------------------------------|------------------------|
| **Calm years** | We pay to maintain protection (carry, financing, trading costs) | Fund value **drifts lower**. This is the insurance premium. |
| **Stress begins** | Protection becomes more valuable as spreads widen | Fund value **rises** |
| **Full crisis** | Spreads can move from roughly 50 to 60 bps to 150 bps and beyond | In our models, fund value can reach **ten times initial capital or higher** |

Your **$5 million** is the posted stake. The magnitude of a crisis gain depends on how much protection we carry and whether we **add** exposure as stress builds. We do not promise profits in quiet markets. We do structure the book for **convexity**, meaning modest ongoing cost in normal conditions and very large upside when a rare event occurs.

## A familiar story: Bill Ackman in March 2020

In late February 2020, as COVID-19 began to shut down economies, the hedge fund manager **Bill Ackman** and his firm **Pershing Square** bought credit protection on investment-grade and high-yield bond indices, including instruments closely related to the market we trade. At the time, spreads were still relatively **compressed**, near **fifty basis points** on investment-grade indices. Markets had not yet priced a global shutdown.

Within weeks, panic took hold. Companies drew on credit lines, earnings forecasts collapsed, and investors rushed for safety. CDX investment-grade spreads widened sharply, moving toward **one hundred fifty to two hundred basis points** in the March turmoil. The protection Ackman had bought **rose dramatically in value**. Pershing Square reportedly turned a position that cost on the order of **tens of millions of dollars** in premium into a gain of roughly **$2.6 billion** over a matter of weeks.

That trade is the archetype of what we are trying to replicate in structure, if not in exact scale. Ackman was **already long protection before** the worst of the move. He did not need to predict the virus. He needed to be **insured when credit repriced**. When CDX IG spreads rose, mark-to-market gains on long protection flowed straight through to fund value.

Our fund follows the same economic logic on **CDX.NA.IG**. We hold protection in calm conditions so we are positioned **before** headlines arrive. When spreads widen in a crisis, our net asset value rises with them. In our backtests on the COVID episode, the best pyramid configurations produced modeled peak multiples in the **mid-teens** on the posted collateral, consistent with the same spread dynamics that made Ackman's trade famous.

## When it triggers: two layers

Our preferred structure is a **pyramid**, which combines a calm-market base with a stress add-on.

### Layer 1: Ready in calm markets

When credit spreads are **low** (below roughly sixty basis points, a complacent market), we **enter** long protection and **remain** in the trade through ordinary volatility. That is how we aim to be **in the position when a crisis begins**, rather than buying insurance only after the news has already moved markets.

### Layer 2: Additional protection in stress

When spreads **cross a stress threshold** (roughly **eighty to one hundred basis points**, when credit is clearly under pressure), we **add** a second layer of protection, effectively doubling exposure. We **reduce** that add-on when spreads **ease back** toward more normal levels or after a **fixed number of days**, so we are not paying for crisis-size exposure longer than necessary.

In quiet times the fund carries **one unit** of protection. In the early phase of a crunch it can carry **two**, which is when the payoff curve is steepest. If no crisis occurs during the five-year term, holding costs will likely consume most or all of the posted capital. That trade-off is central to the mandate.

## What it costs if nothing happens

Catastrophe insurance has a price even when the catastrophe does not arrive. That price is paid through your **$5 million** of posted capital as the fund holds protection in calm markets.

In our historical simulation, a representative setup keeps protection in line with the fund mandate: roughly **$100 million** of index CDS on a **$5 million** fund in quiet conditions. Against **your** capital, the model shows typical calm-period drag of:

| Period | Drag on your $5M fund (model) |
|--------|-------------------------------|
| **Per month** | about **$50,000 to $90,000** (roughly **1% to 2%** of fund value) |
| **Per quarter** | about **$150,000 to $265,000** |

The core component is the **insurance premium** itself. At calm spreads near sixty basis points, carry alone is about **$50,000 per month** for every $100 million of protection, which equals about **one percent per month** of a $5 million fund.

We do **not** run a passive position at maximum size through every quiet month. The pyramid rules are designed to **keep costs down**:

- We **enter** only when spreads indicate a calm market.
- We **exit the base leg** once stress is established (for example near **one hundred fifty basis points**), which trims exposure after the initial widening phase.
- We **time-limit the stress add-on** and close it when spreads retreat, so we do not carry double exposure longer than the rules require.

In the same backtest at comparable sizing, a passive position with **no exit rule** cost roughly **thirty percent more** in calm markets than our disciplined pyramid approach.

Over a full five-year term with **no** crisis, these costs can still consume much or all of the **$5 million**. That is the nature of the product.

## What “ten times or more” means

**Target:** If a severe credit dislocation occurs during the fund's life, peak value during that episode should reach **at least ten times** your **$5 million**, which is **$50 million or more** on the modeled crisis path.

That objective requires **scaling protection up when stress arrives**, not running at maximum size through every calm month. In crisis windows our models add exposure and produce peak multiples well above ten times on the fund. In calm months we keep the line closer to mandate size so the cost figures above stay proportionate to your $5 million stake.

In our historical simulation from 2011 through 2026, with full trading costs included:

- When protection is **scaled in stress**, the best pyramid configurations reached roughly **twelve to fifteen times** peak crisis multiples on major episodes.
- On the **COVID-19** credit shock, modeled peaks reached roughly **thirteen to nineteen times**, depending on configuration.
- About **half** of five-year fund start dates in the test **would have reached ten times** when a qualifying crisis occurred.

COVID was a real event but a **moderate** dislocation by our full-crunch design standard. A larger widening, closer to a deep systemic crisis, is what the fund is sized to capture.

## Who this is for

**Ultra-high-net-worth investors** who:

- Already hold diversified wealth and can **afford to lose the full $5 million**
- Want **asymmetric upside** linked to a **macro credit event**, not individual stock selection
- Accept that the fund is **in the market before panic** and **adds** exposure only when stress is confirmed, which improves the odds relative to reacting after the fact

This is **not** a savings account, a steady-return hedge fund, or a speculative crypto position. It is a **term commitment** that pays when credit fear reprices sharply.

## Risks you should understand

**Calm markets create ongoing cost.** On your $5 million fund, quiet months typically draw down roughly **$50,000 to $90,000** in our model. Over several years without a crisis, that can consume much or all of the posted capital.

**You can lose the entire posted amount.** The fund is structured as **option-like premium**, not principal-safe capital.

**Past simulations are not guarantees.** Real crises differ in speed, liquidity, and legal terms.

If losing **$5 million** would materially affect your financial security, **this fund is not appropriate for you.**
