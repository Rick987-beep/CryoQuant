







France Tail Risk: Convex Trades on a French Sovereign Stress










France Tail Risk


Convex trades on a French sovereign stress · 28 September 2026 · Research note, not financial advice




The thesis in brief.


First, nobody is calling France safe: the cost of insuring French debt is at a multi-year high, and traders are already betting against French government bond futures. What the market does not yet price is the possibility that the European Central Bank (ECB) hesitates to defend French bonds when it is needed.


Second, a French debt restructuring or a euro exit is, in my view, well below a 1% risk, and it cannot be bought cleanly. The road toward it can be traded, however: a widening gap between French and German bond yields, a sell-off in French banks, and a standoff with the ECB.


Third, the most plausible large move is a repricing similar to Italy in 2018, most likely in the first half of 2027. In that case the yield gap would widen to 2 to 3 percentage points, and French bank shares would fall by 30 to 40%.


Finally, the best "convex" trades are put spreads on French banks and options on the French–German yield gap, expiring around December 2027. A convex trade is one where the possible gain is many times the amount at risk.




1. Where things stand


The yield on the ten-year OAT (Obligation Assimilable du Trésor, the standard French government bond) has risen to about 4.7%, its highest level in 18 years. The gap to the German ten-year Bund (the German government bond, the euro-area benchmark for safety) exceeds 110 basis points (bp; 100bp equals one percentage point). That is the widest gap since 2012. The price of five-year CDS (credit default swaps, insurance-like contracts that pay out if a borrower defaults or its debt loses value) has reached about 52bp a year, so insuring €10 million of French debt now costs roughly €52,000 annually.

The underlying problem is that France's primary balance (the budget balance before interest payments) is roughly −3.7% of GDP. Debt therefore keeps rising even before interest costs are counted, and debt service is expected to increase by about €10 billion in 2027. Prime Minister Lecornu has announced savings of about €54 billion to hold the 2027 deficit at 5% of GDP, against roughly 6.5% without measures. The bank ING considers that target not credible.


Not all of the recent move is French, though. Yields are rising worldwide: the US 30-year Treasury yield touched 5.5%, its highest since 2004, and the ECB has raised interest rates twice this year to 2.5% because the war with Iran has pushed up inflation. A strategist at the French bank Natixis says that much of the OAT sell-off reflects this global trend, while some investors are also positioning specifically for French stress by selling OAT futures short. CDS on French banks are at their highest since April 2025, and the shares of Crédit Agricole and Société Générale have gained only about 1% and 2% this year, compared with roughly 19% for the European bank index.

Historical templates


In 2018 the yield gap between Italian and German bonds widened from about 163bp in January to 340bp in October, after a populist government clashed with the European Union (EU) over its budget. Insurance on Italian government debt and on Italian banks became more expensive at the same time, a classic "doom loop" in which weak banks and a weak government drag each other down. In 2022 the shares of Monte dei Paschi fell 46% within weeks, while the bank sector fell about 15%, before the ECB had a tool to cap yield gaps. France's finances are in worse shape than Italy's were in 2018, but its investor base is deeper. One strategist expects buyers to step in at around 200bp, which is why I see 200 to 300bp, rather than 500bp, as the plausible stress zone.

The mechanism


French banks and insurers hold large amounts of OATs, and under EU rules such holdings carry no capital charge. When yields rise, the bonds lose value, which hurts the banks' balance sheets and raises their funding costs. Weaker banks raise fears that the state would have to step in, which pushes government yields up again. Insurers are a second channel: sovereign bonds make up about 12% of insurers' and pension funds' holdings, and many life-insurance contracts can be redeemed at any time, so forced selling is possible.


The circuit-breaker is the ECB. Its bond-buying backstop, the Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI), is conditional on fiscal compliance and a sustainable debt path, and the ECB itself decides whether a country qualifies. A government elected on a program that conflicts with Brussels is exactly the case in which that decision turns political. One analyst framework treats yield gaps of 120–150bp as the zone in which the TPI would be activated, and expects it to pull them back toward 80–90bp.

5. What is tradeable


Some instruments are exchange-traded (listed), meaning anyone with a normal brokerage account can use them. Others are over-the-counter (OTC), meaning they are negotiated privately with a bank under an ISDA agreement (the standard contract from the International Swaps and Derivatives Association) and are open only to professional investors.




Instrument
Pays on
Access and issues


Euro-OAT futures (FOAT) against Euro-Bund futures (FGBL), both traded on the Eurex exchange
The yield gap widening
Listed and very liquid. The trade is linear, so the gain is proportional to the move. It also has "carry" (the ongoing cost or income of holding a position): being short the gap costs roughly the gap itself, about 1.1% a year.


Options on OAT futures; options on Bund futures
Moves in bond prices
Bund options are liquid, but options on OAT futures trade thinly, so prices can be poor.


France five-year CDS; the iTraxx SovX Western Europe index (a basket of European sovereign CDS)
The cost of French default insurance rising
OTC, so an ISDA agreement is required. EU rules restrict buying such protection without owning a related risk. CDS also would not pay out if France redenominated its debt, because under the 2003 ISDA definitions a G7 country changing its currency is not a credit event. It is therefore a bet on price moves, not a hedge against an event.


iTraxx indices of senior financials, subordinated financials and Crossover (junk-rated companies), using "payer" options
Broad stress in European credit
OTC and liquid. A payer option gains when the index spread widens, but French names are only a small share of each index, so the exposure to France is diluted.


Options on BNP Paribas, Société Générale (GLE), Crédit Agricole (ACA), the SX7E (the Euro Stoxx Banks index) and the CAC 40
A fall in bank or equity prices
Listed and easy to access. Option prices on banks are probably already elevated after the recent sell-off, so check the "skew" (the extra price of downside protection) before buying.


Euro/Swiss franc puts; gold call options
The euro weakening or the euro's survival being questioned
Listed or OTC. They are the only instruments here that pay out cleanly if redenomination becomes a real possibility.


Prediction markets such as Polymarket
A snap election, or Macron or Lecornu leaving office
Thinly traded and covers only the political trigger, not the market damage. I found no market on a debt haircut or a euro exit, and I would distrust any quote for one. Check whether they are legal for you in Germany.




I would avoid French banks as OTC counterparties, because the risk that your counterparty fails exactly when your protection pays out is obvious.


6. The most convex trades

Put spreads on French banks
Buy a put option about 10–15% below today's price (OTM, out of the money) and sell one 35–40% below, expiring December 2027. Selling the deeper put makes the position cheaper, while the gain in a stress is many times the premium. Add a small number of puts more than 30% out of the money as a lottery ticket. Société Générale has historically been the most sensitive of the big banks; BNP Paribas is the most liquid; the SX7E is cheaper if single-stock option prices are too high.

2
Options on the OAT–Bund yield gap
OTC "payer" options that pay if the gap widens beyond 200–300bp, bought from a dealer that is not French. They are cheap because the market assumes the ECB will cap the gap, and they are the purest bet that it will not. If you can only trade listed products, hold a small FOAT/FGBL futures position instead.

Only if permitted and properly hedged (see section 5). The running cost is about 52bp a year. My rough arithmetic suggests gains of about 4, 8 and 17 times one year's cost if the spread rises to 100, 150 and 250bp.

4
Breakup tail
Nine- to twelve-month euro/Swiss franc puts and gold calls, well out of the money. They cost little and pay out most in an extreme scenario, in which the value of the euro itself is questioned.
Crisis and breakup




The strike matters more than the instrument.
 A put 30% below the current price pays only in the crisis case. If the stress case is the central scenario, the main position must be closer to the money. As a guide, a widening of the OAT–Bund gap to 200bp gains about 7 times a year of the futures' carry cost (duration of about 8, meaning the price sensitivity to a one-point yield move, multiplied by 90bp, compared with roughly 1.1% in carry).


Sizing.
 I would budget roughly 1 to 1.5% of net asset value (NAV, the total value of the portfolio) per year for premiums and expect most of it to expire worthless. An illustrative split is about half in bank put spreads, a third in yield-gap options or the futures position, and the rest in the tail trades. Staggering expiry dates avoids depending on a single date.


Exit rule.
 The ECB is the party that ends this trade. Once it signals action, yield gaps tend to snap back quickly, so I would take profits at the first credible sign of TPI activation.


What I would skip




Bets on a debt haircut.
 There is no liquid market, so any quoted price would be unreliable.


An outright short position in OATs.
 It is mostly a bet on global interest rates, it costs money to hold, and buyers tend to appear when yields are high.


Trades timed to rating-agency dates.
 These events are largely priced in, and the earlier downgrades caused little reaction.




7. Caveats




I could not obtain live option prices or implied volatilities, and volatility after this sell-off is probably high. Please get quotes from your broker or dealer before sizing anything.


All scenario probabilities and payoff multiples are illustrations, not forecasts.


Please check the EU restrictions on uncovered sovereign CDS, and your ISDA documentation, with your compliance adviser.


A correct thesis can still lose money if the timing is wrong, because protective positions lose value while you wait.
