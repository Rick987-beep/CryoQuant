01 / Executive Summary

France is repricing. The spread trade compounds with it

France has become the fault line of the euro area. Its ten-year government bond, the OAT, yields 4.85%, near the highest level since 2002. The premium over German Bunds has widened from 84 basis points (bp) at the end of August to 138 bp today, after touching about 150 bp on 5 October. France now borrows at a higher rate than Italy, Spain and Greece.

The trigger is political. A minority government has to pass a €54bn consolidation budget through a fragmented parliament before the end of the year, six months before a presidential election that the far-right candidate leads in every poll. The two previous prime ministers fell on their budgets. Euro-area finance ministers told Paris on 8 October to pass the budget to calm markets. ECB President Christine Lagarde reminded them that the ECB's backstop, the Transmission Protection Instrument (TPI), comes with eligibility conditions. France, under an EU excessive deficit procedure, does not meet them today.

The cleanest way to express this view is the spread between French and German ten-year yields. It removes what moves every bond market together (oil, inflation, the ECB's rate path) and leaves the price investors demand for holding France instead of Germany. In 2018 Italy's spread rose from 129 bp to 307 bp in six months during a budget clash with Brussels. We expect France to follow a similar path into the 2027 election.

The trade

Sell Euro-OAT futures and buy Euro-Bund futures in equal interest-rate sensitivity on Eurex. The position earns about €263 for every basis point the spread widens, whether German yields rise or fall. Each time the spread widens by 20 bp, the profits are reinvested and the position grows with the move, while a trailing stop protects the gains. The risk is set at €10,000 per unit and the payout profile resembles a deep out-of-the-money option, without paying an option premium.

The payout

If the spread reaches 300 bp, the level of Italy's 2018 peak, before 1 July 2027, our simulation turns €10,000 into 11.7× on average and 15.2× in the median case. At 240 bp the payout is 4.5×; at 400 bp it is 24.3×. If no stress comes, the average path returns about half the stake.

Why now

We put a 35% probability on the spread reaching 240 bp or more by mid-2027: 30% for an Italy-2018-style stress and 5% for a contested backstop. A standard pricing model at 65 bp of annual volatility put the same outcome at about 7% on 5 October. Markets price French risk as a slow grind; we see a political sequence with a fixed timetable and no backstop on hand. The spread has also eased about 10 bp from its 5 October level, which improves the entry.

What investors should know

  • The view can be right and still lose: a 40 bp pull-back before the main move stops the trade out. In the simulation this happens on 25% of the paths that eventually reach 300 bp.
  • Without a stress, the carry and normal market noise cost about half the stake on average.
  • The stop is managed daily and a weekend headline can move the spread past it.
02 / France

Fiscal arithmetic, political deadlock and a conditional backstop

France combines a persistent primary deficit, a parliament without a majority and an ECB that has raised rates twice this year instead of cutting. Each of these could be managed on its own. Together they explain why the spread has moved at crisis speed.

The speed of the move matters as much as the level. The spread was 84 bp on 28 August. In the third quarter the French ten-year yield rose by the most in a single quarter since 1987, and on 8 October it stood close to 5%. Part of the rise is global: oil prices driven by the US–Iran conflict pushed euro-area inflation up, French inflation reached 3.4% in September, and the ECB raised rates twice. The spread strips out that global component. What remains is the price of France itself, which has risen by about 54 bp in six weeks.

France has run primary deficits for most of the past two decades. With a primary deficit of about 3% of GDP, the debt ratio keeps rising even if interest rates stop climbing, unless nominal growth outpaces the interest bill by a wide margin. It does not. The average cost of the debt stock has risen from about 1.25% in 2020 to around 2%, and each new bond is issued near 5%. With an average maturity of about eight and a half years, every year of record issuance locks higher costs into the budget for a decade. Allianz Trade estimates that stabilising the debt would take savings of more than €80bn, against the €54bn on the table.

The political sequence

Prime Minister Sébastien Lecornu leads a minority government. His 2027 budget freezes public-sector pay and all but the lowest pensions, tightens local-government spending and extends a corporate tax surcharge. On 7 October the Assembly's Finance Committee rejected several of the savings measures before the plenary debate had begun. Finance Minister Roland Lescure has said the government is prepared to use Article 49.3, which passes a bill without a vote but exposes the government to a no-confidence motion. That is how the two previous prime ministers fell. If parliament has not decided within 70 days of filing, the government can enact its budget by ordinance, a route that is legal but politically explosive.

Behind the budget sits the presidential election of 18 April and 2 May 2027. Marine Le Pen of the Rassemblement National polls 31–36% in the first round (Ifop, 30 September) and wins every run-off tested by Toluna Harris Interactive, by 55–45 against Édouard Philippe and 68–32 against Jean-Luc Mélenchon. She is running while appealing a conviction to the Court of Cassation. Her economic programme asks the ECB to bring down French borrowing costs once France "takes back control". A run-off between Le Pen and Mélenchon, both opposed to fiscal consolidation, is the most hostile outcome for bonds. A dissolution and new legislative elections are widely expected after the presidential vote.

The ECB and Europe

The TPI allows the ECB to buy a country's bonds without limit when spreads move in an "unwarranted, disorderly" way. It has never been used. Eligibility rests on compliance with the EU fiscal framework, the absence of severe imbalances, a sustainable debt path and sound policies, and the ECB alone decides. France has been under an excessive deficit procedure since July 2024 and its fiscal path does not match the agreed consolidation. On 8 October officials made clear that no euro-area institution was ready to step in and that the uncertainty was for French politicians to resolve. Apollo's chief economist expects the ECB to wait until spreads threaten financial stability. A backstop that is real but late and conditional is exactly the gap this trade is designed to capture.

Every euro-area crisis has followed the same sequence: adverse fiscal or political news, wider spreads, pressure on banks and politics, then a negotiation between the national government, the Eurogroup and the ECB over who pays. Bondholders lost money only when the official sector diagnosed insolvency (Greece). They were paid in full when the ECB treated the episode as a crisis of confidence (Italy). France sits closest to Italy. The spread trade does not need a default; it needs the negotiation phase, when spreads widen before the backstop arrives.

03 / Model and Assumptions

11.7× the stake if the spread reaches 300 bp

We expect the spread to reach at least 240 bp before 1 July 2027 and take 300 bp as the base-case exit. The trade is evaluated by simulating 10,000 paths of the spread for each level, with realistic noise, costs and timing, and running the trading rules on every path.

Why 240 to 300 bp

Italy in 2018 is the template. A populist government clashed with Brussels over its budget, the ECB had no instrument tailored to the situation, and the spread rose 2.4-fold in six months to just over 300 bp before a December compromise. France starts from a weaker fiscal position than Italy did in 2018, with a deeper investor base. A move of the full 2018 size, 178 bp, would take France from today's 138 bp to about 316 bp; half that move already reaches about 227 bp. We therefore expect 240–300 bp in a stress. We set the base-case exit at 300 bp, the level at which pressure on the ECB is likely to become impossible to ignore. Above 300 bp the trade is in the contested-backstop scenario, where the payout grows further but the risk of an abrupt ECB intervention rises. That is why we take profit there rather than wait.

What the market prices

On 5 October a standard normal-volatility pricing model at 65 bp a year assigned a 7% chance to a spread above 240 bp in June 2027 and 0.5% to one above 300 bp. Eurex options on the OAT future price yield volatility of about 151 bp a year, while the spread itself has realised about 50 bp a year over the last six months. Options are therefore expensive, and the market treats a 2018-style move as remote. The spread trade uses that combination: it needs no option premium and only has to survive spread noise of around 50 bp a year on the way to a move of about 162 bp.

Before the stress, the spread and the Bund yield follow random walks without drift, with daily volatility of 3.18 bp and 3.95 bp and fat tails that allow for occasional large days. The stress itself takes 15 trading days and bridges the spread to the target, with twice the normal noise. During the stress Bunds rally by 10% to 70% of the widening, which covers both a 2018-style move and a flight to German safety. A second set of paths has no stress at all and shows what happens if the view is wrong. About 190 alternative structures were tested on the same paths, including outright futures, short French equities and Eurex options. The spread trade with a 40 bp stop and reinvestment every 20 bp ranked first at every level.

04 / The Trade

Short Euro-OAT, long Euro-Bund futures, with reinvested gains

One position, two listed futures, one venue. The trade is executed on Eurex through Interactive Brokers (LYNX) and needs no OTC documentation, no option premium and no counterparty other than the exchange clearing house.

Structure

Sell Euro-OAT futures (FOAT)

Eurex future on French government bonds with 8.5 to 10.5 years to maturity, €100,000 nominal. Its price falls when French yields rise. One contract moves about €88 per basis point of OAT yield. Indicative level today about 109.40.

Buy Euro-Bund futures (FGBL)

Eurex future on German government bonds of the same maturity, the most liquid bond future in Europe. One contract moves about €97 per basis point of Bund yield. Indicative level today about 121.14.

Selling three OAT futures and buying three Bund futures gives a position that gains about €263 for every basis point the spread widens. A parallel move in both yields barely changes its value. On real Eurex settlement prices, this position would have made €11,490 between 11 September and 8 October 2026, when the spread widened by 45 bp.

How it compounds

The trade starts with three contracts on each side. Each time the spread widens by a further 20 bp, the position is resized so that a 40 bp pull-back would cost no more than the equity at risk at that point. In the early steps that reinvests all profits, which is what turns a linear futures position into a convex one: the dashed line shows the same entry without reinvestment. Once the position reaches 20 Euro-OAT contracts it stops growing, and the stop then protects most of the accumulated profit. The stop is never moved down and there is no re-entry after it is hit.

Operations

The trade is checked once a day after the close. Eurex has no order book for an OAT–Bund spread, so the stop is executed by closing both legs on the evening it is reached rather than by a resting order. The simulation shows that this daily check gives the same result as a resting stop (11.65× against 11.67×). Both futures are physically delivered, so the position is rolled to the next quarterly contract three times, in early December, March and June, using Eurex calendar spreads. A typical successful run needs about 15 to 20 orders over nine months, most of them during the stress itself.

05 / Risks

A defined budget, and the ways it can be lost

The trade is designed to lose no more than the stake, and to lose it in a controlled way. The main risks are that the stress does not come, that it comes in a way the stop cannot survive, or that it comes too late.

Material risks

  • No stress by July 2027. The budget passes, the election produces a working majority or oil prices fall, and the spread drifts between 120 and 170 bp. The trade then pays carry of about €26 a day and is exposed to noise; the average outcome is 0.53× and many paths lose the full stake. This is the most likely single outcome in our scenarios (65% for relief or grind combined).
  • Right view, stopped out. A 40 bp pull-back before the main move, for example on a budget compromise or an early ECB signal, ends the trade with no re-entry. This happens on 25% of the paths that later reach 300 bp. It is the price of the convexity: a wider stop survives more noise but compounds more slowly.
  • Gap risk. The stop is executed by hand after the close. A weekend headline or an overnight ECB announcement can move the spread well past the stop, and the loss can then exceed the €10,000 budget. Spread volatility 50% above the recent level lowers the average payout at 300 bp to 8.36× and raises the stop-out rate to 46%.
  • ECB intervention. A credible TPI statement or a new purchase programme can compress the spread by 30–50 bp within days. In 2012 Italy's monthly spread fell from 475 bp in July to 349 bp in October after "whatever it takes". The trailing stop and the 300 bp exit are designed to lock in profit before that point.
  • Timing. A stress after July 2027, for example after a summer dissolution, falls outside the horizon. A late stress inside the horizon pays less (7.74× at 300 bp) because carry and noise have had longer to work.
  • Margin and liquidity. Futures margin is posted from account cash and grows with the position. At the 20-contract cap it is a multiple of the original stake. Profits fund it, but the account needs liquidity beyond the risk budget, and the broker can raise margin requirements in a crisis.
  • Model risk. The probabilities are judgements and the simulation uses six months of calibration data. Payouts are model estimates, not forecasts, and historical episodes do not repeat exactly.
  • Operational risk. The trade needs a daily check, three rolls and disciplined execution of the stop. Weekly monitoring lowers the average payout to 9.37×.

What would make us close early

We would exit before the target on a credible ECB statement that France qualifies for the TPI, on the start of TPI purchases, or on a budget agreement backed by a stable majority. Each of these removes the gap between the market's assumption of a cap and the ECB's actual reaction, which is where the trade earns its money.
