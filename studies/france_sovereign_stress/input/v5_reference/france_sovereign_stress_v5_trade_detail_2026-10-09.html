01 / Executive Summary

Two IBKR futures trades for a 240 bp+ OAT–Bund stress

The view: the OAT–Bund 10-year spread, 138 bp today, widens to at least 240 bp by 1 July 2027. The base case is 300 bp, a 2018-style stress or worse. The book is built to be super-convex: lose the €10k if the view is wrong, and compound hard if it is right. Two trades on IBKR / LYNX do this best.

Trade 1 · FOAT / FGBL spread (core)

Sell 3 Euro-OAT Dec 2026 futures and buy 3 Euro-Bund Dec 2026 futures. This is long the yield spread: it earns about €263 for every basis point the spread widens, whatever Bunds do. Stop at −€10k (about 38 bp of narrowing). Each +20 bp of widening, the size is reset to equity ÷ 40 bp and the stop trails. Mean 11.7× at 300 bp, 24.3× at 400 bp.

Trade 2 · Short CAC 40 future (satellite)

Sell 1 CAC 40 future plus 1 mini future (about €86k notional). French equities fall when the spread widens, about 17% per 100 bp in the last two years, partly because of global risk-off. Stop at −€10k (11.7% above entry). Add each 6% fall. Mean 3.4× at 300 bp; lower payout, but its stop-outs happen on different paths.

How to combine them

Run the spread trade alone for the highest payout (11.67× mean, 15.21× median at 300 bp, 25% of right-world paths stopped out). Or put 75% of the budget in the spread trade and 25% in the CAC short: the mean falls to 9.60×, but the share of right-world paths that end with nothing almost halves to 13%. The two payouts correlate only 0.36, because noise stops them out on different days.

What can go wrong

  • No stress by 1 July 2027: both trades bleed carry and noise. The spread trade returns on average 0.53× of the budget, but 61% of no-event paths end with nothing.
  • Right but too early or too noisy: a 40 bp pull-back before the event wipes the position out, and there is no re-entry. This is the price of the convexity.
  • Gaps: IBKR cannot hold a stop on the OAT–Bund spread, so the spread trade is checked once a day after the close and closed by hand. A weekend or overnight headline can move it well past the stop before you act.
  • Workload: one five-minute check a day, an order on the days an add or stop is due (a burst of a few during the event), and seven roll days over nine months. Weekly checks are possible but cost about a fifth of the payout.
  • Margin: at the 20-contract cap the futures need far more margin than €10k. Profits fund it, but check IBKR’s requirement before each add.
  • The CAC short is partly a global risk-off bet and overlaps with the existing French bank trade (BNP Paribas, Crédit Agricole and Société Générale are CAC members).

03 / Trade 1 · FOAT / FGBL Spread

Short OAT futures, long Bund futures: 11.7× mean at 300 bp

Short FOAT / long FGBL pays the spread itself. Bunds rallying or selling off barely matter, because the two legs carry the same € per bp. The only noise the stop has to survive is spread noise: about 50 bp a year, against roughly 160 bp of move to the base case.

Why it is the best candidate

It pays the exact variable of the view, it is cheap to hold (about 6.3 bp a quarter of carry, no option premium), and futures can be resized as gains come in. That compounding with a trailing stop turns a linear spread bet into a convex one.

What it gives up

A stop instead of a premium: a 40 bp pull-back before the event wipes out the position, which happens on 25% of the right-world paths. After the cap, a 40 bp pull-back costs about €70k of profit.

How the trade is run: the short version

You place two orders on day one and nothing else. There is no ladder of resting orders to enter in advance, and nothing to cancel and re-enter when you roll. Instead, you keep a one-page rule card and look at one number, the trade equity, once a day after the close. On most days the answer is "do nothing". You act only when the equity crosses one of two numbers on the card: the stop number (close everything) or the next-add number (make the position bigger). Three times in nine months you roll the futures to the next contract, which takes two orders each time.

This is not a simplification of the model. It is how the model works: it looks at daily closes, and it sizes every step from the equity at that moment. The simulation below shows that a once-a-day check at the close gives 11.65× at 300 bp, against 11.67× with a stop resting in the market. The difference is noise.

Why there are no resting stop or ladder orders

There are three reasons. First, IBKR cannot hold a stop on the spread. A stop has to be attached to one tradable price, and the price we care about is the difference between two contracts. Eurex runs single order books for some bond spreads (Bund–Buxl, BTP–Bund, Schatz–BTS) but not for OAT–Bund, and IBKR's own "Smart" combos that would simulate one only work on US exchanges. Separate stops on each leg would be worse than none: a general bond rally could trigger the FOAT stop alone and leave you with an outright Bund position.

Second, the size of each add depends on the equity at the moment of the add, so it cannot be fixed in advance. Third, every order is tied to a specific contract month. A ladder of orders on the December contracts would have to be cancelled and rebuilt at each roll. Rules written in euros of equity avoid all three problems: they do not depend on which month you hold, and they never need re-entering.

The rule card (this replaces the ladder of orders)

Each row is a state of the trade. You start in the first row. When the trade equity closes at or above the "next add" number, you resize to the position in the next row, and from then on that row's stop and next-add numbers apply. When the equity closes at or below the row's stop number, you close everything and the trade is over. The rows are spaced 20 bp of spread apart and every stop sits 40 bp below the last add, but you never need to look up the spread itself: the equity tells you.

Trade equity = €10,000 plus all realised and unrealised profit and loss of the two legs, including rolls and commissions. The rows assume a clean move; in practice you simply move to the next row when the equity crosses the ‘next add’ number, whatever the date. From Add 5 the size is capped at 20 FOAT contracts, so the stop no longer sits at €0: it sits about €70k below the equity of the last add.

The daily check, step by step

  1. After 17:30 CET, when Eurex has published the day's settlement prices, open the TWS Portfolio window. It shows the unrealised profit and loss of the FOAT and FGBL positions.
  2. Compute the trade equity: €10,000, plus the realised profit and loss already in your log (from rolls, closed contracts and commissions), plus today's unrealised profit and loss of both legs.
  3. If the equity is at or below the stop number on your current row, close both legs the same evening (Eurex trades until 22:00 CET): buy back the FOAT, then sell the FGBL, both with limit orders. The trade is over.
  4. If the equity is at or above the next-add number, move to the next row. Sell the extra FOAT contracts and buy the extra FGBL contracts shown in that row, then write the new row's numbers in your log. Check the margin first with the TWS what-if preview (in the order ticket, click "Check margin"); at the larger sizes the requirement is a multiple of the original €10k.
  5. If neither, do nothing. Check whether the OAT–Bund spread has reached 300 bp; if it has, take profit.

How often you will actually act

On the simulated 300 bp paths the trade is resized 4.2 times on average over its whole life, and about 6 times on the paths that survive to the event. Most of those adds come in a burst. The model's event moves the spread roughly 160 bp in three weeks, which means an add every two or three days. Outside the event the spread drifts about 50 bp a year, so a 20 bp step is a matter of months. In the wrong world there are 0.7 adds on average. Including the entry, the rolls and the exit, a typical right-world trade needs about 15–20 orders in nine months, and a wrong-world trade about 10.

If a daily check is too much, the simulation prices the alternatives. A Friday-only check costs about a fifth of the payout, because in a fast event the adds come too late. Dropping the ladder altogether keeps the stop but loses most of the convexity:

Rolling the futures

Euro-OAT and Euro-Bund futures are physically delivered. If you are short FOAT at expiry, you must deliver French government bonds, and IBKR does not let retail accounts go into delivery: it closes such positions itself shortly before expiry. So every quarter the position has to move to the next contract. This is called rolling. The model assumes a roll in the first three business days of December, March and June. By then most of the market has moved to the next contract, and the old one stops trading on the 8th.

A roll is one calendar-spread order per leg. A calendar spread buys one month and sells the other in a single trade, so you are never without a position in between. Eurex has its own order book for these calendar spreads, which keeps the price fair. In TWS the easiest tool is the Futures Roll Builder. An alternative is SpreadTrader (right-click a contract → Tools → SpreadTrader, then click the price where the Dec row meets the Mar column).

04 / Trade 2 · Short CAC 40 Future

The best equity expression: 3.4× mean at 300 bp

A short CAC 40 future is the best equity way to hold the view. It ranks first among 18 shorts on mean payout and is stopped out far less often than single stocks, because it is less noisy and cheaper to hold.

Why French stocks fall when the spread widens

Channels

Discount rate: the OAT is France's risk-free rate, so long-duration and concession businesses (Vinci, Eiffage, ADP, property) suffer. Balance sheets: insurers and asset managers hold OATs. Fiscal risk: a deficit crisis brings surtaxes and contract cuts. Credit: banks and corporate spreads reprice with the sovereign. Risk-off: spreads widen when global appetite falls.

How much is French?

On the same days the S&P 500 also falls about 8.7% per 100 bp of French spread, against about 17–19% for the CAC. In 2011 the CAC fell no more than the Euro Stoxx; in Italy 2018 the local index fell only about 6% per 100 bp more than Europe. In a France-only stress the CAC probably falls less than modelled. With all stock sensitivities scaled to 60%, the CAC short drops to 2.47×.

The ladder: when to sell more

Every time the CAC falls another 6% from the entry level, you sell more futures, sized from the equity at that moment, and you move the stop down to 12% above the new level. As with the spread trade, the adds are not entered in advance. Each one is decided at the daily check, after Euronext closes at 17:30 CET. Look at the CAC 40 index close. If it has crossed the next level in the table, sell the extra contracts that evening. Then change the resting stop: modify its quantity to the new total and its price to the new stop level (in TWS, double-click the stop in the Orders window, change both fields and click Transmit). That is one sell order and one stop modification per add, and there are only three adds before the cap.

Rolling the futures

CAC futures are cash-settled, so nothing is delivered, but an expiring contract closes your position at the settlement price on the third Friday. To keep the trade on you roll a few days earlier into a later month. Because the mini future (MFC) is listed only one to three months ahead, the simplest scheme is to roll both contracts together into the furthest month the mini offers. That gives four roll dates: 14 Dec 2026, 16 Feb, 13 Apr and 15 Jun 2027. The Futures Roll Builder in TWS does each roll as a single calendar-spread order.

Ticket 2C · Roll (four times: 14 Dec 2026, 16 Feb, 13 Apr, 15 Jun 2027)
Tool
TWS Portfolio window → right-click the FCE position → Roll → choose the furthest listed month (Feb 2027 in December, then Apr, Jun and Jul 2027). Set a limit at the mid of the calendar and submit. Repeat for the MFC position, into the same month
Why the same month
MFC is only listed one to three months ahead. Keeping both contracts in the same month means one stop level and one roll date for the whole position
Then move the stop
The old stop does not follow the roll. It stays on the expiring contract, and if it triggered it would open a new long position. Cancel it, and enter a new buy stop on the new month for the full size, at the old stop level plus the price difference between the new and the old month (usually a few points)

The one thing to remember is the stop. A roll closes the old contract, but the stop order you placed on it stays alive. Cancel it immediately after the roll and place the new stop on the new month. The rule card is in index points, so nothing else changes: the add levels stay where they are.

05 / Why Options Work in Theory but Pay Less

Same view, bounded loss, a fraction of the payout

Options would give the same bet with no stop and a known maximum loss. In theory they work: the payoff is convex by construction, and noise cannot stop you out. In practice they pay a fraction of the futures at today's prices.

In theory: an OAT put if the stress comes early

The 20 Nov OOAT 105.5 put costs 0.75 at the ask (FOAT ≈ 109.40). If the spread reaches 300 bp by 20 November, the put is worth its intrinsic value at expiry:

13.7×
Bunds unchanged (f = 0)
8.0×
Bunds rally 30% of the move
2.4×
Bunds rally 60% of the move

That is comparable to the spread trade's 16.1× for an early event. But it only works if the event completes in the next six weeks, and only when Bunds do not rally too much.

In practice: why options pay less

ReasonNumbers
Volatility is priced highOOAT ATM implied 11.9% price vol ≈ 151 bp a year of OAT yield, against 68–81 bp realized. The market already charges for a stress
The skew makes our strike dearerThe 105.5 put trades at 14.3% implied. At a flat 85 bp realized vol it would be worth 0.06; the market asks 0.75
Time is shortThe 20 Nov expiry covers 31 of 190 business days. In the model the event finishes before it on only 9% of paths. The front-only OGBL call loses everything on 79%
Rolling pays time value againA ladder to Jul 2027 needs four rolls (20 Nov → 22 Jan → 25 Mar → 21 May → 23 Jul); each one buys new premium at implied vol
OAT puts pay on the OAT yield, not the spreadA Bund rally offsets part of the move: the rolled put pays 1.77× / 1.36× / 1.02× for f low / mid / high
Wide quotes105.5 put 0.62 / 0.75: the spread is 19% of the mid
No compoundingA put's notional is fixed at purchase. Futures resize with gains, which is where the spread trade's convexity comes from

When options would win

If OAT implied vol fell toward the realized 81 bp, if the event were expected before a specific expiry, or if a stop-out on noise is unacceptable: the rolled OOAT ladder was never wiped out (0% lost), against 25% for the spread trade.

A role at the margin

25% in the rolled OOAT ladder next to 75% spread trade cuts the right-but-lost share to about 7%, but lowers the mean by about 2.5 points. Swissquote holds the options; IBKR holds the futures.
