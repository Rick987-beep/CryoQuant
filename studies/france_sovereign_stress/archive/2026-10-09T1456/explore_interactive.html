Trading a French spread blowout: an interactive scenario book

On 9 October 2026 French 10-year government bonds yielded 138 bp more than German ones. This book prices one way to trade a widening to 240 bp or more before 1 July 2027: sell Euro-OAT futures, buy Euro-Bund futures in duration-matched size, risk a fixed €10,000, and add to the position as the spread moves your way. Below you find the thesis, the exact trading rules, the inputs, how the simulation works and what the results mean. The interactive section at the end lets you change the rule, the volatility and the costs and see what happens to the payout.

Summary

The trade is long the French-German spread: short Euro-OAT futures against long Euro-Bund futures on Eurex, in equal euros per basis point. It makes money when French yields rise relative to German ones, and it is close to flat when both move together. A stop 40 bp below the last add caps the loss at the €10,000 budget, and every further 20 bp of widening is reinvested into a larger position.

The payout depends on two things: the stress has to arrive, and the stop must survive the noise before it does. If the spread reaches 300 bp at some point before July 2027, a 10,000-path simulation gives a mean of 11.6× the stake. A quarter of those paths still lose: the spread first moves 40 bp against the last add, the stop closes the book, and the later blowout comes too late.

If no stress comes, the trade is a bet on drift. Sixty percent of those paths are stopped out and the median result is the loss of the full stake. The mean of 0.5× comes from a minority of paths that widen far enough without a defined event. Size the trade on the assumption that the €10,000 can go to zero.

The trade idea

The view is that the OAT-Bund spread reprices from 138 bp into a 200 to 300 bp range over the 2026 budget fight and the run-up to the 2027 presidential election, with a smaller chance of a move beyond 300 bp. Four facts support it.

  • Budget. A minority government has to close a gap of about €54 billion by year-end. A failed vote or a confidence motion can force a new government or early elections.
  • Election. The presidential election falls in April and May 2027, inside the trade horizon. Polls put Marine Le Pen first with 31 to 38% of the first-round vote. Analysts see a runoff against Jean-Luc Mélenchon as the worst case for bonds, because both oppose fiscal tightening.
  • No easy backstop. France is in the EU excessive deficit procedure. That makes it harder for the ECB to use its Transmission Protection Instrument, the tool it created in 2022 to buy the bonds of a country whose spread widens without fundamental cause.
  • Price action. The spread widened about 45 bp between 11 September and 8 October 2026. The market has started to price the risk but sits well below the levels Italy reached in 2011 and 2018.

The model prices a market repricing and leaves out default or a euro exit. In each stress path the spread reaches a target level on some day before July 2027, Bund yields fall by part of the move as money looks for safety, and the position closes at the target.

Futures are the most practical vehicle for a private account. FOAT and FGBL are the most liquid listed contracts on French and German 10-year debt, they trade one tick wide through most of the European session, and retail brokers such as IBKR and Lynx offer them. A duration-matched pair isolates the spread and stays close to flat when euro rates move in parallel.

The trade: entries, exits and conditions

The rules below fix every decision in advance. The simulation applies them to each path mechanically, and a trader could run them by hand from a daily close sheet.

Entry and size. On 9 October 2026 you sell FOAT and buy FGBL in a ratio of 0.907 FGBL per FOAT. At that ratio both legs move by the same euro amount for a one basis point change in their yields: €87.58 for one FOAT, €96.57 for one FGBL. The stop sets the size. The book risks all of its equity over 40 bp, so €10,000 buys €250 per bp, which is about 3 FOAT against 3 FGBL.

Adds. Each time the spread has widened another 20 bp since the last add, net of carry, you resize the position to current equity ÷ 40 bp and move the stop to 40 bp below the new level. All profit goes back into the trade. Each add lifts equity by about half. After the first 20 bp the account holds about €15,300 and carries €380 per bp. On a straight move the position reaches its cap of 20 FOAT against 18 FGBL near 238 bp, with €73,000 of equity. Beyond the cap each further 20 bp adds about €35,000.

Stop. The stop is hard and there is no re-entry. Until the cap binds, the book always risks its full equity over 40 bp, so a stop-out after any add loses almost everything built so far. Once the cap binds, the risk stays at about €70,000 while equity keeps growing, and the stop starts to lock in profit.

Execution. Eurex lists no order book for the OAT-Bund pair, so a broker cannot hold a resting stop on the spread. The simulation checks the spread once a day at the close. If the close is at or through the stop, it exits at the close less 1 bp. A close that gaps far through the stop loses more than the stake, which shows up in the results as a multiple just below zero, typically −0.1×. Adds are taken on the same daily close.

Exit. In a stress path you close both legs on the day the spread reaches the exit level. The default is 300 bp, and the fine grid in the interactive section tests every level from 200 to 400 bp in 10 bp steps. Without a stress the position runs to the close of 1 July 2027, unless the stop has triggered first.

Rolls, costs and carry. Both legs roll on the first business day of December, March and June, ahead of each delivery month. Every fill pays €2.35 commission and €5 of bid–ask, half the €10 tick, per contract. Opening 3 against 3 costs about €44, and a roll at the 20 against 18 cap costs about €560. Carry works against the position at about 0.1 bp per business day, close to 19 bp over the full horizon. In the no-event world the spread has to widen by about that much before the trade breaks even.

Inputs and calibration

Market levels. A snapshot taken at 14:56 CEST on 9 October 2026: OAT 10-year at 4.849%, Bund 10-year at 3.469%, spread 138 bp, all from CNBC. Eurex settlement prices for the front two FOAT and FGBL contracts and two years of daily CAC 40 closes complete the data.

Contract risk. A 20-day regression of each future's settlement price on its cash yield gives the euro value of one basis point: €87.58 for FOAT and €96.57 for FGBL. Their ratio, 0.907, sets the hedge. Carry comes from the price gap between the front and the next contract: 0.114 bp a day on FOAT and 0.015 bp on FGBL, so the spread position pays about 0.1 bp a day.

Volatility. The last 126 business days of closes give a daily standard deviation of 3.18 bp for the spread and 3.95 bp for the Bund yield, with a correlation of 0.14 between them. The window includes the September and October widening, so the estimate leans high. A window that ends before September gives 2.40 bp a day for the spread and runs as the "calmer volatility" preset.

Costs. Lynx charges €2.00 per Eurex futures contract and Eurex adds an exchange fee of about €0.27, so the model's €2.35 per side is slightly conservative. Swissquote Luxembourg also charges €2.00 per contract, with a €9.95 minimum per order. FGBL usually trades one tick wide in European hours and two to three ticks overnight. Neither contract trades at weekends.

Check against the live position. Three FOAT against three FGBL held from 11 September to 8 October 2026 marks to +€11,490 on Eurex settlements, a 44.8 bp widening. The model's contract values reproduce that figure.

How the simulation works

Each run draws daily paths from 9 October 2026 to 1 July 2027. Daily moves come from a fat-tailed distribution (Student-t with 4 degrees of freedom) scaled to the measured volatility, with no drift. Every exit level and every preset reuses the same random draws, so a difference between two results comes from the rule or the level and not from sampling luck. The headline figures use 10,000 paths. The interactive charts use 1,500 to 2,000 paths to keep this file small, and their numbers differ from the headline by a few hundredths.

The same draws feed two worlds.

  • No-event world. The driftless paths run to the horizon and the trade closes on 1 July 2027 if the stop has not triggered.
  • Stress world. For each path the model picks a completion day at random, anywhere from the 15th business day to the horizon. Over the 15 business days before it, the spread follows a bridge that lands exactly on the exit level, with twice the usual daily noise along the way. Bund yields fall by a share f of the jump, drawn between 10% and 70%, to reflect money moving into Bunds. The trade exits on the completion day.

Because the two legs are duration-matched, the Bund share barely matters: split into thirds by f, the mean payouts at 300 bp are 11.6×, 11.5× and 11.7×. Timing matters far more. Paths whose stress completes in the first third of the horizon average 15.7×, and those in the last third 7.7×. A late stress leaves more noisy days during which the stop can trigger, and more days of carry.

What the numbers mean

Every result is ending equity divided by the €10,000 budget. A multiple of 15× means the account ends at €150,000. 0× means the stake is gone. −0.1× means a stop filled through its level after a gap and the loss ran about €1,000 past the stake.

Outcomes fall into two groups with almost nothing in between. Paths that survive until the stress end high, and paths that hit the stop before it end near zero. That split puts the median above the mean: at 300 bp the median is 15.2× and the mean 11.6×. "Stopped out" counts paths that end at 0.1× or below, that is, with €1,000 or less.

The stress-world figures are conditional. They assume the spread reaches the exit level before July 2027 and say nothing about how likely that is. For an unconditional figure, weight the two worlds with your own odds. With a 35% chance of a stress that reaches 300 bp and 65% of no event, the blended mean is 0.35 × 11.6 + 0.65 × 0.5, about 4.4×. The study's own scenario odds (relief 15%, grind 50%, stress 30%, contested 5%) are recorded in the configuration and feed none of the charts.

The pyramid produces most of the payout. The same entry with a stop and no adds averages 3.7× at 300 bp, against 11.6× with adds.

The tighter rule earns about the same mean with far more stop-outs, so it is a worse trade for the same thesis. Volatility matters most: daily moves 50% larger cut the mean from 11.6× to 8.2× and nearly double the share of paths stopped out. A resting exchange stop would change almost nothing, so the daily-close assumption is not driving the results.

The CAC 40 short pays far less in the stress world but holds up better without one, with a mean of 0.8×. A book of 75% spread trade and 25% CAC short averages 9.5× at 300 bp, with a median of 12.4× and 14% of paths stopped out. The payouts of the two sleeves have a correlation of only 0.35 across paths, so one often survives where the other is stopped out.

Risks, in order of importance

  1. Stop-out before the stress. A quarter of the stress paths lose the stake on the way to 300 bp. With daily moves 50% larger, close to half do.
  2. No stress at all. Without an event, 60% of paths are stopped out and the median result is the loss of the stake.
  3. Margin. At the 20 against 18 cap the initial margin is far above €10,000. The simulation funds the adds from open profit, and a broker may not credit all of it in time. A real account needs spare cash or a lower cap.
  4. Execution in a sell-off. The model charges a constant half tick. In a disorderly French sell-off FOAT will trade wider, and size at the close may not be available. Weekend news arrives as a Monday gap.
  5. Shape of the stress. Each stress path lands exactly on the exit level and the trade exits there. Real spreads overshoot and retrace and seldom stop at a round number. The model has no overshoot-and-retrace path.
  6. Contract values. The euro value per basis point comes from a 20-day regression, without cheapest-to-deliver analytics, and drifts as yields move. The hedge ratio needs checking at each roll.

What to watch

The French budget has to pass by the end of 2026, and a failed vote or a confidence motion is the nearest catalyst. The December contracts stop trading on 8 December 2026, so the first roll falls on 1 December. The presidential election in April and May 2027 is the largest scheduled event inside the horizon, which ends on 1 July 2027.
